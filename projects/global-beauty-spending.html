Global Beauty Spending Analysis

An interactive R Shiny project exploring how beauty spending varies with economic development, gender inequality, and female labor-force participation across 50 countries.

At a glance

Two views of the same market

Spending per person and spending relative to household income answer different questions. Comparing both is a useful starting point for market research, subject to the quality of the underlying estimates.

Question

Do countries with similar levels of economic development and gender equality still differ in beauty spending, and what regional patterns appear?

What I did

Integrated country-level socioeconomic and beauty-spending data and built a Shiny dashboard with scatter plots, trend lines, Pearson correlations, box plots, regional filters, and a bubble-chart view.

Tools

R · Shiny · ggplot2 · dplyr · readr · viridis · ggrepel

Result / insight

Exploratory analysis suggested higher-income countries generally had higher beauty spending per capita, while lower-income countries often devoted a larger share of income to beauty spending. Regional differences remained visible. These are descriptive associations, not causal effects.

What this means for comparison

Spending per person and spending as a share of household income describe different aspects of a market. Comparing both helps avoid treating high absolute spending as the only measure of consumer demand.

Data limitations

Beauty-spending values are project-level estimates manually compiled from secondary market sources. The sample covers 50 countries in 2023; the figures are not a standardized official global dataset and do not establish causal effects.

Data and analytical choices

The 2023 project dataset combines GDP per capita, the Gender Inequality Index, female labor-force participation, and two beauty-spending measures for 50 countries. Socioeconomic indicators were drawn from World Bank, UNDP and ILOSTAT sources; spending estimates were manually compiled from secondary market reports. Region labels follow the original Shiny app.

How I would use the findings

Use the comparison to identify markets for closer investigation, then validate category demand, local pricing, distribution costs and source comparability before making an entry or pricing recommendation. This project does not measure market profitability.
